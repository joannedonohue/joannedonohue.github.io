ECOMMERCE × AI / SELF-INITIATED CONCEPT

Commerce,
clarified.

Concept prototype · Illustrative inputs · No live AI connection

A merchant doesn’t need another dashboard. They need to know what deserves attention today, what to do next, and how much confidence to put in the answer.

The customer & the problem.

Initial customer hypothesis: a small ecommerce team juggling growth and inventory. Sales, conversion, and stock data live in separate views. The opportunity is a short, evidence-linked brief that helps the team choose one action, rather than adding another list of alerts. This hypothesis still needs merchant interviews.

Try the decision layer.

Change the inputs below. This rule-based prototype shows how priorities change before introducing a language model.

A store snapshot

Inputs stay in your browser. Thresholds are illustrative, not industry benchmarks.

YOUR NEXT INVESTIGATION

Check replenishment before accelerating demand.

9 days of stock may leave little room for supplier delays. Confirm lead time and committed inventory before increasing acquisition spend.

Missing evidence: supplier lead time, SKU-level stock, and scheduled receipts.

Priority rule: stock under 14 days is evaluated first. A signal to investigate, not an automatic purchasing decision.

Where AI earns its place.

A future version would turn validated metrics and retrieved store policies into a plain-language brief, link every claim to its source, and ask for missing context. Calculations and priority rules would remain inspectable. AI would help explain the evidence; it would not silently change prices, inventory, or ad budgets.

The smallest useful release.

Start with one merchant, a read-only export, and three decisions: inventory risk, return friction, and conversion investigation. Defer autonomous actions, multi-store support, and chat until the brief proves useful. Interview merchants about their last real decision, then test whether they can act on a brief without reopening five dashboards.

How I would evaluate it.

Customer value: time to a useful decision, percentage of briefs rated actionable, and repeat use. AI quality: numerical accuracy, source support for every claim, and correct abstention when evidence is missing. Guardrails: unsupported recommendations, stale data, and mistaken confidence. Conversion or revenue lift would require a controlled test, not a claim based on prototype clicks.

The tradeoff I would watch.

A short brief can hide uncertainty. I would prefer one useful recommendation with visible evidence gaps over three confident-sounding suggestions. The first evaluation set should include conflicting signals, stockouts, missing fields, and seasonal spikes.
