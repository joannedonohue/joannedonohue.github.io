EXPEDIA CANADA / SPONSORSHIP MEASUREMENT

From the seventh inning
to the next booking.

On Expedia Canada’s brand marketing team, I worked on measuring our Toronto Blue Jays sponsorship, connecting game attendance with Expedia conversion in the two weeks after a game.

The business question.

Expedia was a major sponsor of the Blue Jays. The question was whether that investment translated into customer action beyond the stadium. I worked with the team and a partner to connect the physical event with subsequent digital conversion and evaluate the sponsorship investment.

The distinction that matters.

What the window tells us.

It connects an event audience with subsequent conversion during a defined period. That is more useful than counting attendance alone.

What it cannot prove alone.

Game attendees may differ from other travelers before exposure. Attendance also does not establish that every person noticed the sponsorship. A two-week conversion window alone cannot isolate incremental bookings.

Measurement has to earn the causal claim.

A credible incrementality estimate needs a defensible comparison and a way to account for pre-existing differences. This page does not claim a specific control design or lift result for the historical study.

How I would frame the evaluation today.

I would first confirm the audience definition, match coverage, comparison population, and booking definition. Then I would examine pre-period behavior, repeat attendance, other media exposure, and how the result changes with different observation windows. These are my current evaluation priorities, not a claim about every step of the original implementation.

Why this belongs in my product portfolio.

The work connected a business investment, a measurement partner, offline signals, and an online customer outcome. That is the kind of translation I bring to data products: start with the decision, understand how the evidence was produced, and make its limits clear.
