EXPEDIA × SPARK NEURO / CREATIVE EFFECTIVENESS

A little tiger.
A lasting impression.

We partnered with SPARK Neuro to understand what viewers noticed, felt, and remembered across our advertising. Expedia’s zoo spot is one of my favorites: a joyful travel story that made the brand part of the memory.

Big adventure. Small tiger suit.

01 / THE QUESTION BEHIND THE CREATIVE

Remember the feeling.
Remember who made it possible.

An emotional ad can be easy to love and hard to attribute to a brand. We wanted viewers to remember Expedia and travel, not simply a charming child, a tiger, or a story they might mistake for another category’s advertising.

WHAT WE LEARNED

Introduce Expedia early.
Bring it back at the end.

In our testing, showing the Expedia logo in the first few seconds and again at the close was the most memorable approach. The branding gave viewers an early anchor, then reinforced the connection after the emotional payoff.

Qualitative finding from my experience on the Expedia brand team. Historical scores, sample sizes, and comparison cuts are not reproduced here.

02 / HOW WE TESTED

Watch the ad. Measure the response.

We brought a group of participants into a sensor-based study and showed them a range of ads. Working with SPARK Neuro, we examined attention, emotional response, and what people remembered. That gave the creative discussion evidence beyond whether someone said they liked an ad.

03 / WHY THIS STORY STAYED WITH ME

The emotion belongs to the journey.

THE HOOK

A child who lives as a tiger.

The costume makes the story playful and instantly recognizable. Her excitement gives the audience someone to root for.

THE CONNECTION

Travel makes the wish real.

The airport, car, and hotel are part of getting her to the zoo. The travel category is built into the plot rather than added as an unrelated end card.

THE PAYOFF

A memory with Expedia attached.

The encounter with a real tiger pays off the journey. Our takeaway was that people remembered Expedia as the travel brand behind the experience, rather than confusing it with a car ad or another category.

Creative interpretation and qualitative recollection from my work. Explore the commercial on iSpot ↗.

04 / THE SCIENTIFIC JUDGMENT

A strong signal still needs interpretation.

Triangulate the evidence.

A physiological response does not identify its cause by itself. Excitement, surprise, and discomfort can all involve arousal. The scene, viewing behavior, and participants’ answers help explain what the signal means.

Separate the study from a sales test.

A panel watching ads is not automatically a randomized control group. Creative testing helps explain attention, emotion, and recall; it does not by itself prove incremental bookings. That calls for a separate effectiveness study.

The questions I would bring to a creative-test readout
  • Were ad order and repeated exposure accounted for?
  • Was Expedia recalled without a brand prompt, or only recognized after one?
  • Did branding appear during an attentive moment?
  • Did viewers associate the story with travel as well as the brand?
  • Were differences consistent across participants and large enough to matter?

These are interpretation checks, not a reconstruction of our study protocol.

05 / THE PRODUCT LESSON

Make the experience memorable.
Make the value unmistakable.

This is why I love the intersection of creative and measurement. The story can be warm, funny, and human while the evaluation stays disciplined. A product or ad has to earn attention, then make clear what value it delivers and who delivers it.
